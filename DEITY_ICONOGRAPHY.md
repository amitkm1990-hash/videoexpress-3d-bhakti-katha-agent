# Deity iconography — create the gods as in their real, traditional images

Lord Vishnu, Maa Lakshmi, Shesh Naag and Garud must look like the well-known traditional devotional images (calendar and temple-poster art), rendered with a Pixar-style 3D finish. This replaces the older "simplified two-armed" rule of the earlier agent: **Vishnu has four arms.**

Principles

- Real attributes, real colours, real costume. Nothing invented, nothing comic, nothing "cute-ified".
- Adult proportions and serene, dignified, kind faces for every deity. Only Tunnu has chibi proportions.
- 3D finish only: soft global illumination, rich colour, detailed jewellery. Not flat, not photoreal.
- Deities are shown only in the form below, never as statues, idols, paintings or reflections.
- Any distorted, angry, scary or comic face is rejected and regenerated.

| Deity | Must have | Must not have |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Vishnu (Shri Hari)** | Deep cloud-blue skin; serene half-closed lotus eyes, gentle smile; **exactly four arms**: Sudarshan chakra (upper right, spinning on the finger), Panchajanya conch (upper left), lotus (lower right), mace/gada (lower left); tall golden crown; makara earrings; red-and-white U-shaped Vaishnav tilak; Kaustubh gem, vaijayanti garland and Shrivatsa mark on the chest; yellow pitambar dhoti; golden armlets and bracelets; bare chest. Reclining form: lies at ease on the silver coils of Shesh Naag, all four hands visible with the attributes. | Beard, moustache, a fifth or missing arm, peacock feather (that is Krishna), weapons held wrongly, anger, a second Vishnu anywhere |
| **Maa Lakshmi** | Golden-fair glowing complexion; red silk saree with a wide gold border; jewelled gold crown and jewellery; pink lotus in the right hand; left hand blessing or gently pressing the Lord's feet; **two arms**. Stands or sits on a pink lotus. | Four arms, elephants, coins pouring, dull clothing |
| **Shesh Naag** | Giant silver-white divine serpent; **exactly seven fanned cobra hoods**; the large central hood has one kind face with ruby eyes and a small golden crown; gold ornaments on the hoods; thick pearly coils that serve as Vishnu's bed. | Faces on all seven hoods, fewer or more hoods, frightening fangs, a second serpent |
| **Garud** | Eagle-faced divine being with a human torso; golden curved beak; kind bright eyes; small gold crown; two large red-and-gold wings; red-orange body with gold armlets; yellow dhoti; two arms. | Four arms, a plain bird with no human body, a menacing look |

If the model cannot hold seven hoods, five hoods may be used for that clip only if the hood count is recorded in the report; the central hood with a face must always be present.

Reverence rules

- No deity is mocked, made foolish, greedy, hurt, angry or the butt of a joke. A deity may smile or laugh warmly. The comedy comes from Tunnu's loving scolding, the Guru's disbelief and the running "Ekadashi par hi uthenge" refrain.
- The sleeping Vishnu keeps eyes and mouth closed until the awakening clip.
- Nothing sacred (tulsi, conch, lotus, chakra) gets a gag.

## Added for the Bhai Dooj pack

| Deity | Must have | Must not have |
|---|---|---|
| **Yamraj ji (Lord Yama)** | Deep dark blue-green complexion; dignified, kind face with large calm eyes and a thick black moustache; tall golden crown with two small curved horns; crimson-red royal robe with golden border; golden shoulder armour and jewellery; golden danda (staff) in the right hand and the left hand raised in blessing; **two arms** in the simplified cartoon form. Shown as a respected, gentle guest. | Noose or weapon pointed at anyone, a scary, angry or demonic face, a buffalo in frame, skulls, fire, a second Yamraj |
| **Chitragupt ji** | Slim middle-aged scribe; wheatish skin; round kind face with a thin black moustache; tall yellow-and-white turban; yellow kurta with a white dhoti and shoulder-cloth; a thick red cloth-bound ledger (bahi-khata) in the left hand and a long feather pen in the right; gold earrings; **two arms**. | Comic or sinister look, a second ledger-holder, a crowd of clerks |

Reverence rules for this pack

- Yamraj ji and Chitragupt ji are honoured guests. Neither is mocked, made foolish, greedy, angry or the butt of a joke. They smile warmly. The comedy comes from Jamuni's loving bossiness, Bholu's love of sweets and Acharya Somdutt's disbelief.
- No scary death imagery anywhere; the story is about a sister's love and the blessing of Bhai Dooj.
- Nothing sacred (tilak, diya, thali, ledger, danda) gets a gag.
